# Assembling R(4,5) <= 25 as one Lean theorem (plan, 2026-10-28)

Goal: a Lean statement whose only hypothesis is catalogue completeness, with everything else kernel-checked:

    theorem r45_le_25 (hcat : CatalogueComplete) : ¬ ∃ c : Colouring 25, Good 4 5 c

`CatalogueComplete`: for every d in {8, 10, 12}, every (3,5)-good colouring of K_d equals some listed A_i up to a
vertex permutation, and every (4,4)-good colouring of K_{24-d} equals some listed B_j up to a vertex permutation
(the McKay catalogues `r35/n{d}.g6`, `r44/n{24-d}.g6`). This is the one trusted input of the whole route.

## Ingredients and their status

| # | fact | form | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | no good (3,5)-colouring of K_14; no good (4,4)-colouring of K_18 | SR refutations from `monster sr-gen` (37 and 62 lines) checked by the Lean checker: ⊥ in the SR tier means no good colouring (the checker's soundness theorem) | reference-checked; Lean SR rule in progress (part 4) |
| 2 | some vertex of K_25 has even red degree | Lean combinatorial lemma: sum of red degrees = 2 x (red edges), 25 vertices, so not all degrees are odd | to write (part 5a; small) |
| 3 | every vertex has red degree in [7, 13] | Lean lemma from 1: a vertex with >= 14 red neighbours induces a (3,5)-good colouring on 14 of them (no red K3 there, no blue K5); >= 18 blue neighbours induce a (4,4)-good colouring. Needs "induced sub-colouring is good" (Image.lean / Lift.lean have the permutation and restriction machinery) | to write (part 5b; small) |
| 4 | for every d in {8,10,12} and every catalogue pair (A_i, B_j): no good colouring of K_25 has vertex 0 red to 1..d inducing A_i and blue to d+1..24 inducing B_j | per-unit SLRAT lemmas (`monster unit-lean`, assumption rule, Lean `--lemma`), one clause each | d=7,8,9 and the d=10 sample Lean-verified unit by unit; the stages themselves are certified online by the hint checker (d=7,8,9 done, d=10 running, d=12 next) |
| 5 | the assembly: take a good colouring, pick an even-degree vertex (2), relabel it to 0 with reds first (Good is invariant under permutations), d in {8,10,12} by (3), the red neighbourhood is A_i up to a permutation and the blue one B_j (hcat), relabel again, contradict (4) | Lean proof, finite case split over (d, i, j) | to write (part 5c) |

The SR degree-bound proof for K_25 (`sr-gen --bounds`, 124 lines) is the clausal twin of 3: it derives the bounds
after sorting vertex 0's edges. For the assembly the lemma form (3) is more convenient, since it speaks about every
colouring and every vertex, and it reuses 1 instead of re-deriving it inside K_25.

## How 4 is fed to the kernel at scale

300,000 unit lemmas cannot be 300,000 imported theorems. Two options, both streaming through one checker process:

- **Batch lemma mode** `checker --lemmas LIST`: verify the listed SLRAT files one after another in one process,
  accumulating `List (Σ clause, proof that it holds in every good colouring)`; the driver theorem states that every
  clause in the output list holds. The assembly theorem then takes `(hunits : ∀ (d i j) ∈ Index, UnitClause d i j ∈ verified)`
  and the final Lean run closes it by `decide`/`native_decide`-free list membership (the index is generated by
  `monster` from the catalogues and covers, and the same generator produces the expected clause for each (d, i, j)).
- **One combined SLRAT proof**: sub-instances for 1 (needs SR inside sub-instances and the lift rule on their ⊥),
  pure lifted degree-bound clauses, the unit lemmas as assumption sub-proofs, and ⊥ at the end. This makes the
  catalogue-completeness step a clause-level fact ("the red neighbourhood is one of these graphs") that the proof
  cannot express without a canonical-form argument, so it is not the first choice.

Batch lemma mode is the plan. Cost: Lean verification is ~0.4x solve time (RESULTS 2026-10-27 ~19:30), so d=10 is
~800 CPU-h of Lean, d=12 ~1,200 CPU-h; both can be sharded like the solves (one lemma file per unit, streamed, never
stored together: ~260 B/conflict, 25-45 TB for d=10 if kept, so verify-and-delete per unit).

## Parity-free alternative

If 2 turns out awkward, the route can avoid parity by solving d=11 (105 x 1,184,231 pairs) and d=13 (1 x 546,356 pairs)
as well; d=7 and d=9 are already certified. Rough cost with covers: d=13 ~100 CPU-h, d=11 ~2,500 CPU-h (not measured).
The Lean lemma is far cheaper.

## What stays trusted

Only `CatalogueComplete`. Making it a checked step means an enumeration proof (induction on the vertex count with a
canonical-form argument, as Gauthier and Brown did inside HOL4 with a custom solver and nauty); SR "without loss of
generality" lines are the right clausal tool for it. Research item; not needed for the theorem above, where it is an
explicit hypothesis.

## Status 2026-10-28 ~11:00: the driver exists (`checker --assemble`)
Everything above except 1 (which `R35_le_14` / `R44_le_18` cover by `native_decide`) is in Lean and the driver is built:
`monster assemble-spec --d D ... --from-record FILE|--proofs LIST|--fake` writes the untrusted hints (each catalogue graph's
generalisation and the cover's permutation inverted) and a spec block; `checker --assemble SPEC` decodes the g6 files,
reads the hints and the clauses, and evaluates `stageOK` in parallel chunks (SOUNDNESS.md "The assembler"). Forms:
in-process (`proofs`: the assembler verifies the SLRAT files itself, theorem `stageRefuted_of_verified`, no hypothesis),
record (`record`: clauses written by `checker --lemmas --record`, theorem `stageRefuted_of_record`), timing
(`fakerecord`: unverified exact unit clauses, proves nothing, exit 3). Cost of the coverage check alone: d=10, 313 x
130,816 pairs, 229 s wall on 14 threads (1,328 CPU-s), 361 MB. The final run for the theorem: one spec with the three
stages, `record` for d=10 and d=12 (their `verified.rec` from `unit-lean-stage`), `proofs` or `record` for d=8.
